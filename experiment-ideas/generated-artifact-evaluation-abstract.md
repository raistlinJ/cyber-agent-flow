# Measuring the Value of Generated Tools, Skills, and Guidance for Cybersecurity Agents

**Abstract — proposed research**

Language model agents can extend their capabilities through generated tools, skills, and procedural documents, but successful demonstrations alone do not establish whether these additions improve task performance or generalize beyond the interactions that produced them. This research proposes a controlled evaluation framework combining CyberAgentFlow's agent execution and artifact-generation workflows with ScenarioForge's reproducible network scenarios deployed in CORE. The study will compare baseline agents with guidance-only, tool-only, and combined configurations on held-out cybersecurity tasks, initially emphasizing observational activities such as service discovery, reachability assessment, and evidence-based reporting. Artifacts and scenario definitions will be frozen before evaluation, with fresh agent sessions, matched resource budgets, randomized condition order, and repeated trials across supported model seeds. Environment checks will document runtime drift; state-changing tasks will require validated restoration before inclusion in controlled comparisons. Independent verifiers will assess task success against evaluator-only ground truth. Secondary measures will capture finding accuracy, repeated-trial reliability, token consumption, elapsed time, underlying tool operations, operator assistance, and policy compliance. Paired analyses will account for task and scenario grouping and report uncertainty, regressions, and generation and repair costs. Separate development and test scenarios will distinguish reuse of prior solutions from transfer to unfamiliar tasks and network structures. The proposed contribution is an auditable methodology and supporting evaluation infrastructure for determining when generated artifacts improve cybersecurity agents, whether benefits arise from executable functionality or procedural knowledge, and whether those benefits justify their creation and execution costs. The study will distinguish individual artifact effectiveness from the reliability of the artifact-generation process itself.

**Keywords:** cybersecurity agents; generated tools; agent skills; controlled evaluation; reproducibility; network emulation.
